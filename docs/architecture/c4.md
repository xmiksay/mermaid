# C4 — architecture notes

Part of the [mermaid-svg architecture reference](../architecture.md).
Parser: `src/parse/c4/` · Renderer: `src/svg/c4/`.

- **Layout is hybrid** (`uses_graph_layout` in `src/svg/c4/mod.rs`). A
  diagram with more than `GRID_MAX_RELATIONS` (8) *drawn* relations
  (`drawn_relations`: both ends declared and distinct — a relation naming an
  undefined alias is dropped from the drawing without an error, the parser's
  policy being unchanged, and a self-relation is a loop that needs no
  routing, so neither counts), or with an
  explicit `LAYOUT_TOP_DOWN()` / `LAYOUT_LEFT_RIGHT()` / `LAYOUT_LANDSCAPE()`
  statement, uses the layered **graph mode**; everything else keeps upstream's
  row-flow **grid mode**, so small diagrams like `samples/c4.mmd` (4 relations)
  still match upstream (#327/#370). Should the graph construction ever hand
  sugiyama an invalid graph (`LayoutError`), the diagram falls back to the
  grid rather than collapsing. Both modes produce one `Placement` (leaf rects
  and boundary frames, both in declaration pre-order, plus optional
  per-relation routes); `layout_scene` then plans every relation, measures
  shapes + frames + lines + labels (`outline::bounds`, a corner-based `Span`
  kept distinct from the origin-and-size `Rect`) and shifts the drawing to
  the padding before anything is drawn (#396).
  - **Grid mode** (`src/svg/c4/grid.rs`, `grid_placement`): each level flows
    its shapes left-to-right, wrapping a shape row after `SHAPE_IN_ROW` shapes
    *or* once the row would outgrow `SHAPE_ROW_WIDTH_LIMIT` (boundary rows wrap
    only on `BOUNDARY_IN_ROW`), and is sized from that content. The width check
    mirrors upstream's `Bounds.insert`, which wraps as soon as a shape's edge
    would reach `screen.availWidth` (~800px headless): with upstream's default
    `width`/`c4ShapeMargin` that already wraps same-sized shapes after two per
    row (#370). A row is as tall as its tallest shape; shorter shapes stretch
    and centre their text. Column/row gaps grow to fit the widest/tallest
    relation label (`min_label_gaps`); a reversed pair needs its two labels'
    combined width across columns but only their combined height across rows
    (#399), so rows stay compact. A shape with a self-relation keeps loop room
    on both sides (`LayoutNode::reserve`). `$c4ShapeInRow`/`$c4BoundaryInRow`
    only affect this mode. Relations are straight centre-to-centre lines
    (`rel_straight::straight_routes`); several between the same two shapes
    (either direction) run in parallel, spread as far as their labels' extent
    across the line needs — capped so both shifted ends stay inside their
    shapes — so each label sits on its own line; a different pair whose line
    would run on top of an earlier one (a shape and the boundary around its
    neighbour) is nudged aside by `PARALLEL_GAP`, and lines leaving one shape
    within 8° of each other (`fans_out`) slide their shared end apart along
    the face. Shifted ends are pulled back inside the *drawn* outline
    (`pull_inside` — the shifts are sized on the bounding box, and a queue's
    pill or a cylinder's caps are narrower near the corners), and ends of
    different relations on one shape keep `END_GAP` (12px) apart: a crowded
    line slides one end, the other, or both across itself until clear. A straight line that would
    run through a foreign shape or a boundary's header text detours instead
    (`grid_route::detour`, after every straight line is placed): A* over an
    8px grid of the free space between rows and columns — cells within 8px
    of another relation's line cost extra, and the line-of-sight
    simplification may not cut along one — drawn as a spline with doubled
    waypoints, leaving and entering its end shapes square-on (`square_off`).
    A detour whose end would land on another line's end aims that end
    off-centre instead, so two detours into one shape never share their last
    stretch.
    A boundary with a relation to one of its own members gets
    extra bottom padding (`rel_loop::enclosure_room`, both modes) so that
    relation's run holds its label clear of the arrowhead.
  - **Graph mode** (`src/svg/c4/graph/`, `graph_placement`): boundaries are
    compound clusters laid out bottom-up (`graph/tree.rs` numbers them in
    pre-order; `Pass::run` sweeps children first). Each boundary's direct
    items — shapes and already-laid-out child frames — get their own
    `sugiyama` pass, and the padded frame (header band on the screen-space top
    plus `HEADER_ROUTE_ROOM`, grown to `header_min_w`) takes part in the
    parent's pass as one node. A frame therefore encloses exactly its members,
    and sibling frames or foreign shapes can never overlap it by construction
    — flowchart's post-hoc `resolve_subgraph_overlaps` only separates sibling
    frames, which is why C4 doesn't reuse it. Nesting works to any depth.
    - **Relations as edges**: a relation is an edge in its endpoints' deepest
      common cluster (`rel_path`); in every boundary between that and an
      endpoint it is an edge to a point-sized **port** pinned onto the
      boundary's first or last layer (`sugiyama::layout_pinned` /
      `LayerPins`, which rejects a pin with an edge into its pinned side), so
      the inner pass threads it between the members to the frame edge. Each
      relation keeps its own layout edge and dummy chain
      (`Layout::edge_polylines`), so relations lifted onto the same pair of
      boundaries don't share a stretch. A frame spans its ports and every
      dummy waypoint across the flow, so routes only ever leave through the
      top or bottom.
    - **Orientation** (`graph/orient.rs`): only edges inside a strongly
      connected component are ever reversed. Small components (≤ 12 items)
      are ordered by an exact dynamic programme over subsets: each reversed
      relation costs 1 (parallel relations count individually, so the
      majority direction wins) and placing an item before one fed from nearer
      the graph's sources costs 1.5, so a source chain such as Events →
      Detector → Workflow keeps running down even where breaking it would
      save one reversal. Ties go to the item nearer the sources, then the
      earlier-declared one; bigger components use greedy Eades–Lin–Smyth.
      Ranking uses `LayoutConfig::pull_sources` (applied after pinning, so a
      source feeding only bottom ports joins its siblings' layer), and
      crossing minimisation runs 48 sweeps plus `LayoutConfig::transpose`
      (adjacent-swap refinement).
    - **Port sides and order** (`Pass::settle`): a port goes on the side of
      its boundary facing the relation's other end, and ports on one side are
      ordered by where that other end lies across the flow (`LayerPins::order`
      fixes the order of a pinned layer; crossing minimisation arranges the
      inside around it), so lines leave a frame in the order they need
      outside it. Both are only known once the parent is laid out, and port
      sides change the inner layering, so `settle` guesses (outgoing below,
      incoming above), then re-runs with the observed sides and order until
      the sides stop changing; a side that has flipped twice is frozen, so
      this ends within `MAX_SIDE_PASSES` (`settle_checked` reports whether it
      converged; the fixture tests assert it does).
    - **Stitching** (`graph/route.rs::assemble`, `graph/frames.rs`): each
      level's piece is cut where it leaves its frame (`until_exit`) —
      straight above or below its last point inside, never slanting along
      the edge towards the port — and continues with a short perpendicular
      stub (`push_stub`, doubled so the curve reaches it before bending and
      never skims the edge). Crossings of one frame edge are kept
      `EXIT_GAP` apart (`route::FrameMarks::claim_exit` slides a later one
      along the edge, with its trailing points). An outer level continues from the inner frame's
      stub: first straight out of the inner block's layer band (`bridge` — a
      frame shorter than its band would otherwise let the route cut across
      the frames beside it), and straight down through the corridor to its
      own frame when nothing lies in between (`drop_straight`). A piece that
      would cross the boundary's header text detours past it (`avoid_header`,
      one slot per detour, each slot its own corridor depth (`track_depth`:
      `HEADER_TRACK` apart, then interleaved, never repeating); it first rises straight to the corridor under the
      header, dropping any points between header and corridor, so the turn
      never doubles back or slants along a shape's top). `through_bands` turns each dummy into a vertical
      pass through its layer band (doubled band edges plus approach/depart
      points, matched by containment since `bow_opposite_pairs` nudges
      reversed-pair dummies off-centre), and `leave_bands` makes a piece
      starting or ending on a shape — or on a boundary that is the relation's own
      endpoint — leave that shape's whole band first, then
      continue straight on a little (`depart`, short so the middle of the
      gap stays free for the staggered tracks) — the B-spline passes a single
      control point at `(prev + 4p + next)/6`, so collinear runs keep it out
      of neighbouring shapes while the gaps get smooth S-bends instead of
      straight diagonals. `graph/ends.rs`: `spread_ends` fans out every end
      meeting one face of a shape (with the straight run leaving it) up to
      `MAX_END_SPREAD` apart — shifts are worked out on the unshifted routes,
      so a route that is one straight run is moved once, not by both ends —
      and lines heading sideways turn at staggered depths (`TRACK_GAP` apart,
      in staircase order so they don't cross), with the layer gap
      grown to hold the busiest shape's fan-out. `graph/runs.rs` applies the
      shifts: an end's straight run can be long (a dummy column through
      several layers), and the column beside it was never checked by the
      layout, so only the part of the run that stays clear of every foreign
      shape (`Scenery`; members of a boundary the relation ends on don't
      count) and doesn't land on another route's same-direction frame
      crossing moves — the route jogs back to its own column after it.
      `snap_slivers` merges sub-pixel waypoint slivers the shifts leave.
      Shifted runs also keep clear of every header's text (`Scenery::headers`).
      Relations between the same two shapes fan out in the order their
      routes part (`first_divergence`), so a bundle never crosses itself.
      `graph/legs.rs::bow_apart` finally finds legs of two routes running
      within 3px the same way (within ~6°) — long shallow legs between bands
      can meet that way — and bows the later one off by a doubled control
      point, or, next to a leg's fixed end, makes it come in along the flow
      axis. Each shape is widened along
      its in/out face
      so its ends get at least `MIN_END_GAP` (`Pass::face_need`; only bites in
      left-right layouts); `ease_ends` makes routes leave and enter along the
      flow axis.
    - Relations are drawn with `curve_basis_path`; label placement and
      canvas measurement use the same curve flattened by `curve_basis_points`.
      The layer gap grows to fit the tallest (TD) or widest (LR) relation
      label; left-right layouts wrap labels narrower (`RelLabel::new(_, true)`)
      to keep that gap down. A shape with a self-relation reserves loop room
      on both sides of its layout box.
  - **Direction**: `LAYOUT_TOP_DOWN()` / `LAYOUT_LEFT_RIGHT()` /
    `LAYOUT_LANDSCAPE()` (C4-PlantUML's alias for left-right) parse into
    `C4Diagram.layout_direction` (`C4LayoutDirection`, last statement wins,
    also inside a boundary body). Top-down is the graph-mode default.
    Left-right uses flowchart's `LR` transform: every size is swapped going
    into sugiyama and every coordinate coming out (`graph::Dir::swap`), so
    boundary headers stay on the screen-space top. Previously these
    statements were silently swallowed by the parser's tolerant unknown-line
    arm.
  - **Duplicate aliases**: the first declaration in source pre-order is the
    relation endpoint in both modes (`Tree::home`,
    `rel_route::endpoint_shapes`); later duplicates are still drawn.
    `Tree::item_in` walks cluster parent links, never aliases, so a member
    reusing its boundary's alias can't loop.
- **Shape sizing** (`src/svg/c4/text.rs`, `wrap.rs`): shapes keep upstream's
  `BOX_W` width but have no fixed height — `layout_shape_text` wraps the
  stereotype, name, `[techn]` and description into one block (description
  directly under the technology at normal spacing; person glyph
  `PERSON_GLYPH_H` between stereotype and name; no `[techn]` line for an empty
  technology), `shape_size` adds the outline's insets (cylinder caps) and a
  `BOX_MIN_H` floor, and the drawing code (`shapes.rs`) renders that very
  layout, so text never overflows the box on any side. A queue is drawn as
  upstream's `h-cyl` shape (`rendering-elements/shapes/tiltedCylinder.ts`), a
  cylinder lying down: end caps are ellipses of half-height `ry = h/2` and
  half-width `rx = ry / (2.5 + h/50)` (`text::queue_rx`, always below
  `QUEUE_RX_MAX` = 25). `draw_queue`, the clip outline (`Outline::HCylinder`)
  and the text inset all use that one function. The text wraps
  `4 * QUEUE_RX_MAX` narrower than a box's — the caps' widest — so the wrap
  never depends on the height it produces, even when a grid row stretches
  the queue. Lines wrap by
  *measured* width (`wrap::wrap`, `metrics::proportional_width`: per-glyph
  classes — narrow, lowercase, digits, capitals, wide `M`/`W`, CJK and emoji at
  a full em or more — at each line's own font size and weight) against the
  box's inner width; a token wider than a line breaks after `.` `/` `-` `_`
  `:` `?` `&` `=` (the mark stays on the upper line) or, failing that, by
  character. External elements
  are solid gray (palette, not a dash); the diagram title has no italic
  sub-caption. `header_min_w` measures the boundary title and its `[kind]`
  line at the sizes they are drawn (font + 2 bold, 10 italic); the wider of
  the two, plus `HEADER_TEXT_CLEARANCE`, is the header obstacle every route
  stage avoids (`BoundaryBox::header_text`).
- **Relations** (`src/svg/c4/rel.rs`, `rel_route.rs`, `rel_straight.rs`,
  `rel_loop.rs`; labels in `rel_annotate.rs`, `rel_place.rs`, `rel_label.rs`,
  `rel_text.rs`): `plan_rels` resolves
  endpoints first-declaration-wins, clips each layout route to the *drawn*
  outline of both shapes (`src/svg/c4/outline.rs`: rounded box corners, the
  queue's and cylinder's elliptical caps, boundary frames — a bisection against each convex
  outline's `contains`), and the `c4-arrow` marker's `refX` equals its tip so
  the arrowhead touches the border without a gap or overshoot. Two kinds of
  relation are planned last, so they can keep clear of every other end:
  a **self-relation** (`self_loop`) is a spline loop on the cheapest face —
  right, left, top, bottom — scored by what it would hit (another shape or a
  header, another relation's end or line, a boundary border); a second loop
  on a face nests `LOOP_NEST` outside the first (only where the face is long
  enough), and layouts reserve room for the nesting. A relation between a
  **boundary and its own member** (`enclosure_route`), either direction,
  runs straight from the member's outline to the nearest frame side that
  crosses no other shape or header and runs along no other line, sliding
  along that face away from other ends, and preferring a run long enough to
  hold its label (its height on a vertical run, width on a horizontal one)
  clear of the arrowhead; `rel_loop::enclosure_room` pads the boundary's
  bottom for it in both modes. When every straight way out is blocked, it
  detours to the frame by A* (`enclosure_detour`) instead of crossing a
  shape or header. Labels wrap by
  measured width (`RelLabel`: 200px, 125px in left-right graph mode; long
  tokens broken like shape text) instead of truncating with `…`, on one opaque `edgeLabelBackground` rect covering every
  line (#260). `place_label` (`rel_place.rs`) slides a label along its drawn
  line from the arc-length midpoint (`arc_path::ArcPath`), then
  sidesteps it — at most 24px and never so far that its own line stops
  passing under it, nor to a spot where a foreign line is nearer than its own,
  so a label always reads as its own line's. A spot must clear every shape
  (endpoints included — #366), every boundary header band, every
  already-placed label (#381) and every arrowhead (the last `ARROWHEAD` 14px
  of each arrowed end padded by `ARROWHEAD_PAD`, `end_zone`; grid gaps are
  sized to leave room for it); among those, the first that also covers no
  other relation's line (#382; a relation between the same two elements is
  excluded as running alongside by construction) and no *firm* obstacle — the
  last 24px of every line (its arrowhead end, both ends of a `BiRel`) and a
  3px band along every boundary border — wins, otherwise the cheapest by
  covered firm area, covered line length and sidestep distance. Shapes,
  headers, arrowheads and labels are **hard**: no stage ever accepts a spot
  overlapping one. When every on-line spot covers another line, a spot up
  to 40px beside the line that covers none wins instead; when no on-line spot
  clears the hard obstacles at all, the search widens beside the line (up to
  320px, `off_line`); failing even that, the label goes below everything
  placed (`beyond`). A label off its line gets a thin dashed **leader** from
  its edge to its line (`RelPlan::leader`). Room for labels is sized per *bundle*: the
  relations joining one pair of elements stack their labels along their
  lines, so grid gaps and the graph layer gap fit a bundle's combined label
  heights (`text::bundle_label_extent`). All obstacles
  sit in one uniform-grid index (`Obstacles`, segments tagged with their
  relation), so each candidate only tests what is near it — 400 relations
  place in well under a second. All lines are drawn before all labels.
- C4 supports the full `{System,Container,Component} × {Db,Queue} × {_Ext}`
  element matrix; the `_Ext` variants reuse the same shape with the gray
  external palette. `UpdateElementStyle` / `UpdateRelStyle` /
  `UpdateLayoutConfig` are stored on `C4Diagram` (`element_styles`,
  `rel_styles`, `layout`) and applied at draw time: element `$bgColor`/
  `$fontColor`/`$borderColor`, rel `$textColor`/`$lineColor`/`$offsetX/Y`.
  `$c4ShapeInRow`/`$c4BoundaryInRow` override the grid-mode wrap counts
  (ignored in graph mode). `C4Relation.direction` (`Rel_U/D/L/R`) is parsed
  but used by neither layout mode. `Rel_Back`
  reverses the arrow (from/to swapped at parse time so the head lands on
  `from`; `C4Relation.reversed` remembers it, and `UpdateRelStyle` matches
  the pair as written, `written_pair()`, as upstream does); `RelIndex(index, from, to, …)` is the C4Dynamic step form — the
  leading index shifts every positional slot by one and is prepended to the
  label (`"{index}: {label}"`). Both were previously swallowed by the tolerant
  unknown-line arm.
  C4Deployment's `Node(...)`/`Node_L(...)`/`Node_R(...)` boundary openers alias
  `Deployment_Node` in `parse_boundary_open` (checked `Node_L`/`Node_R` before
  the bare `Node` prefix), so their children nest instead of leaking to top
  level. A boundary/`Deployment_Node`'s optional third arg (`type`) lands on
  `C4Element.boundary_type` and, when present, overrides the fixed per-kind
  `[label]` header tag (`boundary_tag_text` in `src/svg/c4/mod.rs`) — e.g.
  `Deployment_Node(n, "Web Server", "Ubuntu 16.04 LTS")` shows `[Ubuntu 16.04 LTS]`.
  - C4 element/relation macros are **not** positional-only: `split_macro_args`
    (`src/parse/c4/calls.rs`) pulls the `$descr`/`$techn`/`$sprite`/`$tags`/
    `$link` keyword args out of the arg list before slotting the positional
    ones, so a `$sprite=` placed before the positional technology no longer
    shifts every later field. `$descr`/`$techn` override their positional slot;
    `$sprite`/`$tags`/`$link` land on `C4Element`/`C4Relation` (rendering
    deferred). `UpdateBoundaryStyle(alias, $bgColor/$fontColor/$borderColor)`
    fills `C4Diagram.boundary_styles` (reusing `C4ElementStyle` via
    `parse_style_directive`) and restyles the boundary frame fill/stroke/label
    in `draw_boundary_rect`. `SHOW_LEGEND()` sets `C4Diagram.show_legend`
    (consumed; legend rendering is a follow-up).

- **Layout invariants** (`src/svg/c4/tests/`): `invariants::check_scene`
  runs every rendering check on the issue sample, the fixtures in
  `tests/fixtures/c4/` (both directions for the probes) — frames hold their
  members; labels clear shapes, headers, each other and every arrowhead
  (`lines::check_arrowheads`); ends sit on the drawn outlines; no line runs
  through or along a shape, doubles back or crosses header text; and no two
  relations share a stretch longer than 4px within 2px running the same way
  (`lines::check_no_shared_stretch`). `outlines.rs` reads the queue's and
  database's paths back out of the SVG and checks their caps lie on the clip
  outline. `corpus.rs` holds 120 seeded random (plus the seeds wider runs
  caught)
  small diagrams (both modes and directions, nested boundaries, queues and
  databases, self-loops, member/boundary relations, long unbreakable text) to
  every one of those checks — `MERMAID_SEED=<n> cargo test print_corpus_diagram
  -- --ignored --nocapture` prints one. `stress.rs` checks only "no line
  through any shape" on two generated 100-shape / 200-relation diagrams,
  cheap enough for debug CI.
